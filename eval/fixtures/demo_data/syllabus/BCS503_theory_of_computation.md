# BCS503 Theory of Computation (sample syllabus notes, written for this demo)

Credits: 4. CIE 50 marks, SEE 50 marks. Textbook: John E. Hopcroft, Rajeev Motwani, Jeffrey D. Ullman, Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages and Computation.

## Module 1: Finite Automata
Introduction to finite automata, structural representations, automata and complexity, central concepts: alphabets, strings, languages. Deterministic finite automata (DFA), nondeterministic finite automata (NFA), equivalence of DFA and NFA through subset construction, finite automata with epsilon transitions.

## Module 2: Regular Expressions and Languages
Regular expressions, finite automata and regular expressions, conversion of DFA to regular expression, applications of regular expressions, algebraic laws. Proving languages not regular using the pumping lemma for regular languages. Closure properties and decision properties of regular languages, equivalence and minimization of automata.

## Module 3: Context-Free Grammars
Context-free grammars (CFG), parse trees, derivations, leftmost and rightmost derivations, ambiguity in grammars and languages, applications of CFG. Normal forms: elimination of useless symbols, epsilon productions and unit productions, Chomsky normal form (CNF) and Greibach normal form (GNF).

## Module 4: Pushdown Automata
Definition of pushdown automata (PDA), languages of a PDA, acceptance by final state and by empty stack, equivalence of PDA and CFG, deterministic pushdown automata. Pumping lemma for context-free languages, closure properties of context-free languages.

## Module 5: Turing Machines and Undecidability
Turing machine (TM) model, notation, instantaneous description, programming techniques for Turing machines, extensions to the basic Turing machine, multitape Turing machines. Undecidability: a language that is not recursively enumerable, an undecidable problem that is RE, the halting problem, Post correspondence problem.
